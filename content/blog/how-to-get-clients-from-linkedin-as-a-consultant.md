Most LinkedIn advice for consultants amounts to "post every day and be authentic". Most consultants try it for three weeks, get forty likes from other consultants and zero enquiries, and stop. The problem is not the frequency. It is that the profile is a CV, the posts are opinions, and nobody is being messaged.

Here is what actually gets clients from LinkedIn for a consultant in India, in three hours a week. It is stage five of the seven-stage journey, Launch, and it works only if you have already [packaged an offer](/blog/how-to-price-consulting-services-india). LinkedIn amplifies whatever you are selling. If that is "strategy consulting", it amplifies nothing.

## Part 1: The profile is a page, not a CV

Clients who land on your profile decide in five seconds whether you solve their problem. So the profile has the same four things as [the one-page website](/blog/do-you-need-a-website-to-start-a-business).

**Headline: who you help and the result.** Not "Founder | Strategist | Speaker". Write "I help D2C brands under ₹1 crore fix cash flow in 90 days" or "Websites that bring patients to clinics in tier-two cities". The headline appears next to every comment you leave, so it does most of your marketing.

**About section: the offer, then the proof, then how to book.** Three short paragraphs. The offer in one sentence with a price or a "from" price. One real result with a number. A line saying "message me here or book a call at [link]".

**Featured section: one link.** Your page, or your calendar link. Not five articles and a slide deck.

**Photo and banner.** A real photo of your face, well lit. The banner repeats the headline in large text. That is all.

## Part 2: Two posts a week that show proof

Not daily. Two well-made posts a week outperform seven rushed ones, and they leave you time to do the part that actually gets clients, which is messaging.

Both posts follow one rule: **specific proof beats general opinion**. Four formats that work for consultants in India:

1. **The before and after.** "A clinic in Nashik had zero online enquiries in March. Here is the one-page site we put up and the eleven enquiries it brought in April." Numbers, a screenshot, one lesson.
2. **The teardown.** Take a common mistake in your field and show the fix. "Three things on most CA firm websites that send clients to the competitor next door."
3. **The decision.** "A founder asked me whether to kill her lowest-margin product. Here is how we decided in twenty minutes." Consulting sells decisions, so show yourself making one.
4. **The number.** One statistic from your own work, with what it means. "Across 2,500 websites, the ones with a price on the page booked three times more calls."

Avoid: motivational quotes, reposts with "so true", and anything about your journey unless it ends in a lesson the client can use. Peers like those posts. Buyers do not read them.

Put the link to your page in the first comment, not the post. LinkedIn shows posts with external links to fewer people.

## Part 3: Ten messages a week

This is the part that gets clients, and the part most consultants skip because it feels like selling. It is not selling. It is the same referral message that works everywhere else, adapted.

Each week, find ten people who fit your customer description. Use the search filters: title, industry, company size, location. Look for founders and decision makers, not other consultants. Then send one of two messages.

**To someone you know or share a connection with:**

> Hi [name], I've started helping [who you help] with [the result]. Who do you know who's dealing with that right now?

**To a stranger who has posted about the problem:**

> Hi [name], saw your post about [their specific problem]. I help [who you help] with exactly that. If it would be useful, happy to share what has worked for two clients in a similar spot. No pitch.

No attachment, no calendar link in the first message, no "I'd love to hop on a call". Offer something useful and let them ask.

Expect three replies from ten messages, and one conversation a week that leads somewhere. Over a month that is four conversations, which is enough for one client at a decent close rate. The full version of that conversation is in [how to get your first client](/blog/how-to-get-your-first-client).

## Part 4: Comment where your customers are

Fifteen minutes a day. Find five posts by founders or decision makers in your customer group and leave a comment that adds something specific, not "great post". Your headline appears next to every comment, so a useful comment under a founder's post is seen by exactly the people you want, in a context where you look like the expert.

This is worth more than posting for most consultants in the first three months, because it borrows an audience you have not built yet.

## The weekly schedule

| Day | Task | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Write and post one proof post | 45 min |
| Tuesday to Friday | Five comments a day on customer posts | 15 min a day |
| Wednesday | Send ten messages | 40 min |
| Thursday | Write and post the second proof post | 45 min |
| Friday | Reply to every message and comment received | 30 min |

About three hours. Do it for twelve weeks before judging it. LinkedIn rewards consistency, and most people quit in week three.

## What not to do

- Do not buy automation tools that send hundreds of connection requests. LinkedIn restricts accounts for it, and the replies you get are worthless.
- Do not post about "my journey" more than once a quarter.
- Do not pitch in the first message. Ever.
- Do not connect with other consultants and count it as growth.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many followers do I need to get clients on LinkedIn?

None. Clients come from the profile, the messages and the comments, not from follower count. Consultants with 800 connections and a clear offer get more enquiries than ones with 20,000 followers and a vague headline.

### Should I use LinkedIn Premium or Sales Navigator?

Not at the start. The free search filters are enough to find ten people a week. Consider Sales Navigator only once messaging is bringing clients and you want to do more of it.

### What if I have nothing to post proof about yet?

Post the decision format: how you think about a problem in your field, with a worked example. And do one small job for someone you know at a real price, so you have a result to write about within the month.

### Is LinkedIn right for my kind of business?

For B2B services and consulting in India, yes. For consumer coaching, fitness, wellness or anything sold to individuals, Instagram is usually better. The [Fit Score](/fit) will tell you which model you are in, and the platform follows from that.
