Most traffic advice is written for shops and media sites, where more visitors means more money. A one-person service business is different. You do not need ten thousand visitors. You need thirty of the right people a month, and three of them to book a call. Chasing volume is how consultants end up with a blog nobody reads and a page nobody books from.

Here are the sources of the right visitors, without ads, in the order to work on them. This is stage five of the seven-stage journey, Launch, and it assumes you already have [the one page that books clients](/blog/do-you-need-a-website-to-start-a-business). If you do not, build that first. Traffic to a page with no offer is wasted.

## First, what "traffic" means for a service business

Three kinds of visitors reach your page:

1. **People you sent.** You messaged them, or someone referred them, and they clicked your link. Highest intent by far.
2. **People who searched.** They typed a question into Google and found you. Medium intent, but it compounds every month.
3. **People who scrolled.** They saw a post on LinkedIn or Instagram and clicked through. Lowest intent, highest volume.

Most guides start with the third. Start with the first, because it is the only one that works this week.

## Source 1: Direct sends, this week

The fastest traffic is a link you send. When someone replies to [the three-line message](/blog/how-to-get-your-first-client) with "tell me more", you send the page. When a past client asks what you are up to, you send the page. When a WhatsApp group asks for a recommendation in your field, you answer with one line and the page.

This is not scalable and it does not need to be. Ten direct sends a week to the right people beat a thousand random visitors, because every one of them arrives already half-convinced.

## Source 2: Google, over three to six months

Search traffic is slow to start and then it is the most reliable source you will have, because it keeps arriving while you are delivering client work.

It works when your pages answer questions your customers actually type. Not "web design services Mumbai", which every agency fights over, but the questions a clinic owner asks at eleven at night: "how many patients should a clinic get from its website", "do clinics need online booking". Type your customer's problem into Google and look at the suggestions. Those are real searches. Each one is an article.

The rules that matter, from 2,500 websites' worth of watching what ranks:

- **One question per page**, answered in the first paragraph, with the question in the title.
- **Specific beats broad.** "How to price a website for a clinic" will rank and bring the right person. "Web design pricing" will do neither.
- **Write from experience.** Google's systems increasingly reward pages that show the author has done the thing. Numbers from your own work, a screenshot, a client's words.
- **Speed and mobile.** Your page must load fast on a mid-range Android phone on a patchy connection, because that is where most of India reads.
- **Google Business Profile.** If your customers are local, a complete profile with reviews sends calls directly, before the website is ever opened.

Expect nothing for the first eight weeks. Then a trickle. By month six, if you have written eight to twelve good answers, search is usually the largest source of the right visitors.

## Source 3: One social platform, not four

Pick the one platform where your customers already spend time. For consultants and B2B services in India, that is LinkedIn. For coaches and consumer services, Instagram. Not both, not yet.

The pattern that brings clicks is the same on either: post the specific thing you know, with a number or a story, and put the link in the comments or the profile. Generic advice gets likes from peers. Specific proof gets messages from buyers. The [LinkedIn method for consultants](/blog/how-to-get-clients-from-linkedin-as-a-consultant) covers the posting plan in detail.

## Source 4: Answering where the question is already asked

Quora, Reddit, WhatsApp groups, Facebook groups for founders, niche forums. Somebody in each of them is asking the exact question your offer answers. Answer it properly, in three or four sentences, and mention that you wrote a longer piece on it with the link. Two or three of these a week, done well, send a steady trickle of highly qualified visitors for years, because the answers stay up.

The rule is to answer the question first and link second. Answers that are only links get removed and, worse, remembered.

## What to measure

Not visitors. Three numbers, checked once a month in Google Search Console and your analytics:

| Number | What it tells you | Good, for a one-person business |
|---|---|---|
| Visitors who reach the booking link | Whether the page is doing its job | 5% or more of visitors |
| Queries you appear for | Whether search is starting to work | Growing month on month after month three |
| Calls booked per month | The only number that pays rent | Three or more |

If visitors are rising and calls are not, the problem is the page, not the traffic. Go back to [what belongs on the one page](/blog/do-you-need-a-website-to-start-a-business).

## The order, in ninety days

1. **Weeks 1 to 4:** direct sends only. Five messages a week, page link on request.
2. **Weeks 3 to 12:** one article a fortnight answering a real customer question. Six by day 90.
3. **Weeks 5 onward:** two or three posts a week on one platform, and two forum answers a week.
4. **Month 4 onward:** check Search Console, double down on whichever articles are showing for queries, and write their neighbours.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to get organic traffic to a new website?

Three to six months for meaningful search traffic, assuming you publish an answer to a real customer question every two weeks. Direct sends work from day one, which is why they come first.

### Is SEO worth it for a consultant or freelancer in India?

Yes, if you write for your customer's questions rather than for keywords with high volume. A consultant needs a few hundred right visitors a month, not a hundred thousand. Narrow, specific questions are far easier to rank for and bring people who are ready to talk.

### Should I run ads while waiting for organic traffic?

Not before the page converts direct sends. Ads amplify whatever the page does. If ten people you sent did not book, a thousand strangers will not either. Fix the page, then consider a small retargeting budget to people who already visited.
