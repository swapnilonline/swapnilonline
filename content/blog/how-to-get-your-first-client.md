Your first client will not come from an ad. Not from a viral post, not from a job platform, not from a perfect portfolio. Across 2,500 websites built for people at the start of a business, the pattern is almost boring: the first client came from someone who already knew them, or from someone that person knew.

This is stage five of the seven-stage journey. It is the one most people avoid, because it means asking. So here is the asking, made small enough to do today.

## The message

Send this to five people who already know you. Colleagues, ex-colleagues, clients from an old job, the parent from school who runs a business, the cousin who keeps saying "you should do this professionally".

> Hi [name]. I've started helping [who you help] with [the result]. Who do you know who's dealing with that right now?

That is the whole message. No link. No price. No deck. No "let me know if you're interested".

## Why it works when everything else gets ignored

It asks for a referral, not a sale. People who would feel awkward saying "no, I don't need that" find it easy to say "hmm, let me think who". The question is about *their* network, which people enjoy answering. And it gives them a clean way to help you without spending money.

It is also specific. "I've started freelancing, let me know if you need anything" gets a thumbs up and silence, because nobody can picture what "anything" is. "I've started helping small clinics get a website that brings in patients" gives the reader a face to match it to. Half the time, the face is their own.

## Filling in the blanks

**Who you help** is one kind of person, not "businesses". Clinics. Chartered accountants. D2C founders doing under ₹1 crore. New therapists. If you have not chosen yet, read [consulting vs coaching vs services](/blog/consulting-vs-coaching-vs-services) first, then come back.

**The result** is what changes for them, not what you do. Not "web design" but "a website that brings in patients". Not "bookkeeping" but "GST filed on time without a panic every quarter". Not "copywriting" but "a landing page that converts the traffic they are already paying for".

If you cannot fill in the blanks in one sentence, you have a stage three problem, not a stage five problem. Your offer is not packaged yet. Fix the sentence first. The article on [pricing your services](/blog/how-to-price-consulting-services-india) shows what a finished offer sentence looks like.

## The five people

Pick them in this order.

1. Someone who has paid you for anything before, even informally.
2. Someone who has asked you for advice on this topic in the last year.
3. Someone who runs a business and talks to other people who run businesses.
4. Someone senior from a previous job who liked your work.
5. Someone you have been meaning to reconnect with anyway.

Send all five today. Not "this week". Today, before you close this page. The point of five is that it is too small to need a plan and too many to get no reply.

## What to do when someone replies

Three things happen.

**They name someone.** Reply: "Thank you. Would you be comfortable introducing us, or should I reach out and mention you?" A warm introduction converts far better than you contacting the stranger cold, so ask for it.

**They say "actually, I might need this."** This happens more often than you would think. Do not pitch. Say: "Let's talk for twenty minutes and I'll tell you honestly whether I can help." Then book a time, on a calendar link if you have one.

**They say "I'll think about it."** Reply "thank you" and move on. Do not chase. Send them something useful in a month.

## The first conversation

When you get on the call, ask about their problem for ten minutes before you say anything about your offer. What have they tried, what did it cost them, what happens if it stays like this. Then say your offer sentence once, price included, and stop talking. The silence after the price is uncomfortable. It is also where the yes lives. Filling it with a discount is the most expensive habit a new business owner has.

## What counts as done

Someone has paid you. Not agreed. Not "yes, let's do it next month". Paid, even a deposit. Until money has moved, you are still at stage five.

If the five messages get you conversations but no payment, go back one stage rather than forward. Nine times out of ten a stall at Launch is a Package problem: the offer is not clear enough for the other person to repeat it to their friend. Tighten the sentence, then send five more messages.

## Frequently asked questions

### What if I don't have a portfolio yet?

You do not need one for this message. It asks for a referral, not for proof. When a referred person asks for examples, one real result beats ten mock-ups. If you have never been paid, do one small job for someone you know, at a real price, and that becomes your example.

### Should I post on LinkedIn instead?

Do both, but the message comes first. Posting builds an audience over months. The message starts conversations this week. Most first clients in India come from a direct message to a known person, not from a post.

### How many messages before I get a client?

Expect one real conversation from every five messages, and one client from every three or four conversations. So fifteen to twenty messages, sent in batches of five, is a realistic path to a first client for most people with a real skill. If you have sent thirty and had no conversations, the offer sentence is the problem.

### Is it awkward to message people I haven't spoken to in years?

Less than you think. You are not asking them for money or a favour. You are telling them what you do now and asking who they know. Most people are pleased to be asked. The [Launch Call](/call) is where we rehearse this if you would rather not do it alone.
