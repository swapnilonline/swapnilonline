A lead magnet is something useful you give away in exchange for a name and a way to contact the person: a checklist, a scorecard, a template, a short guide. The idea is simple. Most visitors are not ready to book a call today. A lead magnet lets you keep talking to them until they are.

The idea is simple and the execution mostly fails, because most lead magnets are built to be downloaded rather than to be acted on. They attract people who collect free things and never buy. Here is the one rule that separates a lead magnet that brings clients from one that brings a mailing list of strangers.

## The rule: it should do the first step of your paid work

The best lead magnet is not a summary of what you know. It is the first ten percent of what you sell, done for free, so that finishing it makes the person want the other ninety.

A consultant who sells a 90-day cash-flow fix gives away a one-page cash-flow scorecard. Filling it in shows the founder exactly how bad the problem is, in numbers, and the natural next question is "what do I do about it". A coach who sells "your first ten clients in eight weeks" gives away the first-week worksheet: name three people who have asked you for help. A web designer gives away a five-point checklist of what a clinic's website is missing, and the clinic scores itself.

Compare that with "10 Marketing Tips for Small Business", which attracts everyone and moves nobody.

On this site, the lead magnet is [the Fit Score](/fit), an eight-question quiz that tells you your business model and the stage blocking you. It is literally the first thing done on a Launch Call, given away, and it leads straight to the next step.

## Formats that work for a one-person business in India

| Format | Best for | Why it works | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scorecard or quiz | Consultants, coaches | The person diagnoses their own problem and wants the fix | Medium |
| One-page checklist | Services, technical fields | Concrete, quick to use, shows what they are missing | Low |
| Template | Anything with a document at its heart: proposals, plans, contracts | Saves hours; specificity is the value | Low |
| Worked example or teardown | Consultants | Shows your thinking on a real case | Medium |
| Short guide, 8 to 15 pages | All, once you have the others | Depth, and a reason to send a follow-up sequence | High |

Formats to avoid at the start: a 60-page ebook nobody finishes, a webinar that needs an audience you do not have yet, and a "free consultation" offered to everyone, which fills your calendar with people who wanted a free hour.

## How to make one in an afternoon

1. **Take your offer sentence.** The result, for whom, at a price, in a timeframe. If you do not have one, that comes first: [how to price and package](/blog/how-to-price-consulting-services-india).
2. **Write down the first step you do with every paying client.** The diagnosis, the audit, the intake questions.
3. **Turn that step into something they can do alone in ten minutes.** A scorecard with five questions and a score. A checklist with tick boxes. A template with the blanks labelled.
4. **Name it after the result, not the format.** "The Clinic Website Scorecard" beats "Free Checklist". "Your First Ten Clients: Week One Worksheet" beats "Coaching Guide".
5. **Put it on your page behind a short form.** Name, WhatsApp number, email. In India, WhatsApp is the field that matters, because that is where the follow-up will actually be read.
6. **Write the follow-up before you launch it.** A lead magnet with no follow-up is a download, not a lead.

## The follow-up is the product

The download is not the point. The conversation after it is. When someone takes the lead magnet, three things should happen:

- **Immediately:** they get it, with one line telling them what to do with it today.
- **Within a week:** three to five short messages, one a day or every other day, each with one useful idea related to the thing they downloaded. No pitch until the third.
- **On the third or fourth message:** one clear invitation to the next step. A call, a session, a small paid offer.

For a one-person business in India, that sequence works best on WhatsApp with email as backup, because open rates on email in India are low and WhatsApp messages get read. It can be automated cheaply or done by hand for the first fifty leads, which is honestly how most people should start. [What is a sales funnel](/blog/what-is-a-sales-funnel-how-to-build-one) shows how the lead magnet fits into the whole path.

## What "working" looks like

Measure three things, not download count:

- **Opt-in rate:** visitors who take it. Twenty to forty percent of visitors to the page is normal for a specific magnet. Under ten percent means the name or the promise is off.
- **Reply rate:** people who respond to the follow-up. Ten percent is good.
- **Next-step rate:** people who book the call or buy the small offer. Five to ten percent of opt-ins, over a month.

If the opt-in rate is high and the next-step rate is near zero, you built something people want for free but not something that leads to your paid work. Go back to the rule.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best lead magnet for a consultant?

A scorecard or diagnostic that lets the prospect measure the problem you solve. It qualifies them, it makes the problem concrete, and the natural next step is to ask you what to do about the score.

### Should a lead magnet be free?

Yes for the first one. A small paid product, ₹499 to ₹2,000, can work later as a "tripwire" that turns a lead into a buyer, and buyers are far more likely to buy again. But it needs traffic and trust first.

### How do I deliver a lead magnet without expensive tools?

A form that posts to a Google Sheet and an automatic email or WhatsApp message is enough. Free tools cover the first few hundred leads. Spend money on tools only when the manual version has proved the magnet works.

### Do I need a lead magnet to get my first client?

No. Your first client comes from [a direct message to someone who knows you](/blog/how-to-get-your-first-client). Build the lead magnet in month two or three, once people are visiting the page. The [Fit Score](/fit) will tell you which stage you are actually at.
