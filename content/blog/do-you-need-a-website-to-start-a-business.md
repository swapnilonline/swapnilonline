I have built more than 2,500 websites, most of them for people at the very start of a business. So the answer you would expect from me is "yes, and here is a package". The honest answer is different. You need one page. Not a brand. Not a logo suite. Not the twelve-page site you will be "finishing" for a year.

This is stage four of the seven-stage journey, Build, and it is the stage where the most time is wasted, because building feels like progress and asking feels like risk.

## The pattern from 2,500 websites

The people who get their first client within a month put up one page in a week and spend the rest of the month talking to people. The people still "working on the site" a year later started with a brand: a logo, a colour palette, a tagline, six service pages, an about page with a timeline. All of it before a single conversation with a customer.

The website did not get them the client. The conversation did. The website's only job at the start is to make the conversation easier: a link to send when someone says "tell me more".

## The four things on the page

In this order, top to bottom.

### 1. Who you help, in one line

The first thing a visitor should see is themselves. "For clinics in tier-two cities that are losing patients to the hospital down the road." "For chartered accountants who want clients that are not on a price war." If the line could apply to anyone, it applies to no one.

### 2. The offer, with the price

One sentence: the result, for whom, at a price, in a timeframe. "A five-page clinic website, live in 14 days, ₹45,000." The price on the page is non-negotiable for a one-person business. It filters out the people who cannot afford you before they take your time, and it makes the rest trust you more. If you are unsure how to set it, read [how to price your services in India](/blog/how-to-price-consulting-services-india).

### 3. One proof point

One. A result, a review, or a before-and-after. "Dr. Mehta's clinic went from zero online enquiries to eleven a month." A screenshot of a WhatsApp message saying thank you counts. A logo wall of twelve brands you have never worked with does not.

If you have no proof yet, use a promise instead: "If you don't have a paying client by day 90, I keep working until you do." That is the [first-client guarantee](/program) on this site, and it exists because proof takes time and a promise does not.

### 4. One way to book you

A calendar link or a WhatsApp button. Not a contact form.

Contact forms get ignored. People fill them in, nothing happens for two days, they forget. A calendar link starts a conversation at a fixed time. A WhatsApp button, in India, starts one in the next thirty seconds. Both beat "we'll get back to you".

## What not to put on the page

- **A menu of services.** One offer. A menu gets "let me think about it".
- **Your story above the offer.** Nobody reads the about section until they are already interested. Put it below, keep it to three sentences.
- **Stock photos of handshakes.** One real photo of you beats every stock image. If you have no good photo, use none.
- **"Get in touch for a quote".** That is an invitation to negotiate. The price is already on the page.
- **A blog, for now.** Write the blog when you have clients and know their questions. Until then it is procrastination with a publish button.

## Domain, hosting and the ten-minute setup

Buy your name or your trade name as a .com or .in. In India, ₹800 to ₹1,200 a year. Host the page on any simple builder or a free tier of a modern host; the tool does not matter at this stage and switching later takes an afternoon. Total cost, ₹2,000 to ₹6,000 a year.

Spend an hour on the words and ten minutes on the design. The words are the product. A plain page with a clear offer outperforms a beautiful page with a vague one, every time.

## When to build more

When three things are true: you have paying clients, you know the questions they ask before buying, and you have proof to show. Then a second page for a second offer, a page of results, and a blog that answers the questions make sense. Until then, every hour on the site is an hour not spent on [getting the first client](/blog/how-to-get-your-first-client).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use just Instagram or LinkedIn instead of a website?

For the first few clients, yes, if your profile has the four things above: who you help, the offer with a price, one proof point, one way to book. Most profiles have none of them. The advantage of your own page is that you control it, it does not scroll away, and you can send the link in one message.

### Should the page have my rates on it?

Yes. See point two. The only time to hide the price is for bespoke engagements over a few lakh, where "from ₹1,50,000" works.

### Do I need a professional to build it?

Not for the one page. Any builder will do. Where a professional earns their fee is in the words, the structure, and the booking flow. That is why in HOME → BUSINESS the page is built inside the program in week four rather than assigned as homework.
